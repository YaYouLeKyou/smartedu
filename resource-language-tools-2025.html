Assessment and Evaluation

Success Criteria

  • Formative: Peer feedback on tool comparison matrices using a standardised rubric.
  • Sumative: Students submit their completed matrix, recommendation, and a 200-word reflection on learning outcomes.
  • Evidence: At least one measurable improvement (e.g., vocabulary retention score, conversation duration, or error reduction) on a pre/post task.

Differentiation

  • Beginner learners: Pre-select two tools and provide a simplified comparison template.
  • Advanced learners: Require analysis of privacy policies and data handling for each tool tested.
  • Diverse abilities: Allow voice-based, text-based, or visual tools depending on individual needs.

Accessibility

  • Verify that all recommended tools support screen readers and keyboard navigation.
  • Check that tools offer multiple input modes (voice, text, touch) for learners with motor or sensory impairments.
  • Provide offline alternatives for students without consistent internet access.

Data Protection / GDPR Considerations

  • Review each tool's data retention and processing policies before classroom use.
  • Consult your school's Data Protection Officer (DPO) to ensure compliance with GDPR and local data protection laws before students upload personal or identifiable information.
  • Prefer tools that offer EU-based data processing and do not use voice/text inputs for model training.

Limitations

  • The tools listed were prominent in 2025; current offerings may differ in features, pricing, or availability.
  • AI tools may lack cultural depth and reinforce linguistic or cultural biases present in training data.
  • Over-reliance on AI can reduce motivation for human interaction, which remains essential for fluency.
  • Free tiers often impose usage limits that may interrupt learning workflows.

Critical Reflection Questions

Discussion prompts:

  • Can AI truly help learners achieve fluency, or is human interaction essential?
  • What cultural knowledge is embedded in AI language tools, and what might be missing?
  • How do we ensure that AI language learning doesn't promote linguistic imperialism?
  • What are the privacy risks of using AI tools that record and analyse speech?
  • Should AI language tools be required to disclose how student data is used?
