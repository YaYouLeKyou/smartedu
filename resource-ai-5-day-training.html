AI in Education: A 5-Day Training Program

Comprehensive training for educators and youth workers — fundamentals, tools, ethics, and hands-on workshops

Back to Chapter: Teaching
AI Training Beginner to Intermediate 5-Day Intensive Program

Resource Overview

The "Smart Edu AI: A 5-Day Training Program for Youth Workers" is designed to build AI literacy and practical skills among educators, youth workers, and trainees — not to replace them with automated systems. This international program was developed by the Smart Edu AI partnership and delivered in Strasbourg, France, bringing together educators from multiple European countries to share practices and co-create resources.

The program aims to provide practical training through hands-on workshops and group activities, focusing on pedagogical innovation and promoting responsible and sustainable AI use. Throughout the five days, participants learn to use AI as a supportive assistant while keeping the teacher or youth worker at the centre of every pedagogical decision.

"AI literacy is not about becoming a programmer; it is about becoming an informed user who can navigate, critique, and harness AI responsibly."

Audience, Level & Prerequisites

  • Target audience: Teachers, youth workers, trainers, and education support staff.
  • Level: Beginner to Intermediate — no prior AI or programming experience required.
  • Prerequisites: Basic digital literacy (using a web browser, opening applications, joining online meetings). A willingness to experiment with new tools is more important than technical skill.
  • Estimated duration: 5 full days (approximately 35–40 hours including breaks). The program can also be adapted into a series of shorter evening or weekend sessions.

Measurable Learning Objectives

By the end of the program, participants will be able to:

  • Define AI and identify its key technologies used in educational contexts. (Target: score ≥80% on a post-program quiz.)
  • Demonstrate proficiency with at least 5 AI-powered educational tools. (Target: submit working examples of 5 tools with a brief reflection on each.)
  • Apply pedagogical models (e.g., Gagné's 9 Events, 5E Model) to design AI-enhanced learning activities. (Target: submit a lesson plan that integrates at least 2 AI tools with clear pedagogical rationale.)
  • Evaluate AI tools for privacy, bias, and accessibility using a standardised framework. (Target: complete an evaluation sheet for 2 tools with all criteria scored and justified.)
  • Design a follow-up action plan for AI integration in their own institution. (Target: submit a one-page plan with goals, tools, safeguards, timeline, and evaluation methods.)

Programme Structure (5 Days)

Day 1

Foundations: What is AI? Why does it matter in education? Key technologies and practical educational applications. Participants build a shared vocabulary and reflect on their own prior experience with AI.

Day 2

Tools & Pedagogy: Integrating AI into instruction using proven pedagogical models (Gagné's 9 Events, 5E Model). Participants test AI tools for content creation, differentiation, and feedback.

Day 3

Management & Ethics: Project management tools (Trello, Slack), performance tracking, data protection (GDPR), bias detection, and ethical guidelines for AI use in education.

Day 4

Practice & Exploration: Hands-on AI workshops, AR/VR exploration, data analytics, and peer collaboration. Participants build a prototype AI-enhanced learning activity.

Day 5

Future & Action: Innovation contest, project presentations, reflection on the future of AI in education, and creation of a personal or institutional action plan.

Suggested Classroom Activities

Activity 1: Pre-Assessment and Post-Assessment

Duration: 20 minutes (pre) + 20 minutes (post)  |  Materials: AI literacy quiz (10 multiple-choice and 5 short-answer questions), answer key, reflection sheet.

Steps:

  1. At the start of the training, participants complete the pre-assessment to establish a baseline.
  2. At the end of the training, participants complete the post-assessment and compare results.
  3. Participants reflect on the biggest knowledge gains and remaining questions.

Differentiation: Allow extra time for participants who are not native English speakers; provide a glossary of key terms.

Evaluation: Compare pre- and post-scores; calculate average improvement; review reflection sheets for qualitative insights.

Activity 2: Tool Showcase

Duration: 30 minutes  |  Materials: tool showcase template, access to AI tools, scoring rubric, presentation space.

Steps:

  1. Each participant or pair researches and selects one AI tool introduced during the training.
  2. They prepare a 3-minute showcase: what the tool does, a classroom example, an evaluation of educational value, privacy implications, and potential biases.
  3. Participants present and receive feedback using the scoring rubric.

Differentiation: Provide a structured template with sentence starters for less confident presenters; encourage advanced participants to include cost and sustainability analysis.

Evaluation: Rubric (clarity, depth of evaluation, usefulness, critical awareness).

Activity 3: Project Management Simulation

Duration: 45 minutes  |  Materials: Trello or similar board, Slack or similar chat tool, sample project brief, task cards.

Steps:

  1. Participants are given a sample project brief (e.g., "organise a school AI awareness week").
  2. Groups use the project management tools to plan tasks, assign roles, set deadlines, and track progress.
  3. Groups present their plan and discuss how AI could support each phase of the project.

Differentiation: Provide a pre-filled task list for groups that need structure; challenge advanced groups to include risk management and evaluation metrics.

Evaluation: Project plan rubric (completeness, clarity of roles, realistic timeline, integration of AI tools).

Inclusion & Accessibility

  • Provide all materials in multiple formats: printed handouts, accessible PDFs, and slides with captions.
  • Ensure the training venue is physically accessible (wheelchair access, accessible toilets, sign language interpreters on request).
  • Offer translation or bilingual support for participants who are not fluent in the training language.
  • Allow flexible participation: in-person, hybrid, or fully remote options where feasible.
  • Use clear, plain language and visual aids; avoid jargon without explanation.

Privacy, Data & GDPR

  • The training does not require collecting or storing personal student data. Participants should use anonymous or synthetic data when testing AI tools in classroom scenarios.
  • Any AI tools used during the training must be reviewed for data protection compliance. Participants must not enter personal or sensitive data into public AI systems.
  • Under GDPR, educators must ensure that student data processed by AI tools is handled lawfully, with transparent privacy notices and the right to erasure.
  • Participants should discuss data governance policies with their institutions before introducing AI tools into their classrooms.

Limitations & Human Verification

  • The 5-day format provides an introduction, not full mastery. Continued professional development and peer support are essential to sustain AI integration.
  • AI tools evolve rapidly; training content may become outdated. Participants must stay informed through ongoing learning and networking.
  • AI-generated content must always be verified by the teacher before use in lessons. AI is a support tool, not an authority.
  • Not all AI tools are suitable for all contexts. Cost, internet reliability, and hardware requirements can limit adoption in some schools.
  • AI cannot replace the teacher's professional judgement, cultural sensitivity, or relationship with students.

Critical Reflection Questions

Discussion prompts for educators and students:

  • How does a 5-day intensive format compare to gradual teacher professional development?
  • Which AI tools introduced here could be most easily adopted in your context, and why?
  • What challenges might arise when scaling AI use across an entire institution?
  • How can peer learning enhance AI adoption among educators?
  • What support structures are needed to sustain AI integration beyond the training?
